# Stock SH7058 ignition compare paths

The crank-event task table selects the ignition sequence. The sequence
produces a signed timing byte, publishes an unsigned APC command and
hands it to the primary compare scheduler. A later routine supplies the
command plus an interval offset to the secondary compare scheduler.
The compare schedulers and crank interrupt program the SH7058 Advanced
Pulse Controller. The [JK08A output specification](JK08A.ignition-output.md)
describes the corresponding canonical paths.

## JK00C and JK00D

Application vector `0x00020154` selects ICI0A at `0x00029b2a` on both
ROMs. The tooth-7 route tests `0xffff8501 & 0x80` at `0x0002a43e`
and branches past the event call when the bit is clear. The admitted
path publishes the event cylinder index at `0x0002a466` and calls the
event feeder at `0x0002a4c2`. It also computes the single-cylinder
output mask from a nonzero index.

The feeder at `0x00020926` uses the table base `0x00021be8`. Its
loop initializes a count of ten and loads each longword through
`R14 * 4`. It executes `JSR @R6` at `0x000209f4`, increments the
index in the delay slot and decrements the loop count after each return.
The fifth word at `0x00021bf8` selects the ignition sequence.

| Event operation | JK00C | JK00D |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fifth pointer and ignition sequence | `0x00021bf8 -> 0x0004fab2` | `0x00021bf8 -> 0x0004faae` |
| Signed correction builder and store to `0xffff12ee` | `0x0004f05c`, `0x0004f432` | `0x0004f060`, `0x0004f436` |
| Final timing builder and store to `0xffff11e4` | `0x0004f48c`, `0x0004f840` | `0x0004f490`, `0x0004f844` |
| APC command builder and stores to `0xffff11f5` | `0x0004f858`, `0x0004f9a6/0x0004f9b6` | `0x0004f85c`, `0x0004f9aa/0x0004f9ba` |
| Call of `0x0002b51a` and argument extension | `0x0004f9ba/0x0004f9bc` | `0x0004f9be/0x0004f9c0` |
| Separate secondary command routine | `0x0004f9c6` | `0x0004f9ca` |
| Secondary interval input and stored offset | `0xffff23f0`, `0xffff22a0` | `0xffff23f8`, `0xffff22a8` |
| Tail transfer to `0x0002b942` | `0x0004fa06` | `0x0004fa0a` |

The timing correction builder bounds its signed intermediate before
publishing it. The final timing builder reads that correction and
applies the mode-dependent bounds and per-event limits. Its eligible
work-support branch adds a signed byte from `0xffff56c4` or
`0xffff56d8` when the local mode flags have `0x3e` clear. The final
store publishes the selected signed coordinate to `0xffff11e4`.
The periodic snapshot task calls the longword copier at
`0x000213b0`. Its source begins at `0xffff5820` on JK00C or
`0xffff5834` on JK00D. Its destination begins at the signed byte
read by the final timing builder.

On the direct ignition branch the primary command uses
`ROM8[0x000083d8] - int8(final_timing)`, with stock offset 110.
Other stock branches have their own limits and overrides. The call
delay slot uses `EXTU.B R4,R4` to pass the selected byte. The
secondary routine starts from 120 minus its interval input. The
optional trim branch also subtracts `ROM8[0x00008653]` and clamps
negative results to zero. The stock trim byte is zero. The routine
stores the low byte and tail-calls the secondary scheduler with the
sum of the unsigned offset and the published command.

### APC scheduling

Both layouts use the same register addresses and timer instruction
sites. The primary scheduler at `0x0002b51a` splits the command into
a tooth count and fractional pitch. On its ordinary countdown path,
it can move one full pitch from the count into the fractional byte
before applying the gap correction. The stored fields are
`0xffff1594` and `0xffff1596`. The crank handler uses the retained
timestamp at `0xffff15c6` and the normalized period at `0xffff2330`
on JK00C or `0xffff2338` on JK00D for a deferred compare.

The secondary scheduler at `0x0002b942` selects its slot through
`0xffff84fb` bit 7. The slots hold countdown words at
`0xffff1470/0xffff1472` and fractional bytes at
`0xffff1476/0xffff1477`. The scheduler and crank handler both have
GR11B programming paths. In the normal deferred path the handler
scales the selected fraction by the normalized tooth period divided
by ten, caps the delay at 65535 and adds the retained timestamp
modulo 65536. Bit 6 of the APC mode snapshot at `0xffff2551` on
JK00C or `0xffff2559` on JK00D selects alternate scheduler branches.
The periodic snapshot task copies this byte from `0xffff2557` or
`0xffff255f` as byte one of the six-byte copy at `0x000213c4`.
The position diagnostic update at `0x0009ecf4` or `0x0009ece0`
sets live bit 6 when its stock enable is set, the entry mode bits
are clear, both cam record pairs are clear, either of the two
group-`0x18` P0335 records is active and the holdoff is zero.
The tests read bit 7 of byte 1 in each three-byte DTC runtime record.
In the active-mode path, all six record tests clear lets the update
clear bits 6 and 7. The set stores are `0x0009ed84/0x0009ed70`
and the clear stores are `0x0009eef4/0x0009eee0`.

| Compare path | Instruction sites on both ROMs |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate primary compare, GR11A `0xfffff5c2` | `0x0002b702` |
| Deferred primary compare, GR11A | `0x00029fca` |
| Secondary scheduler, GR11B `0xfffff5c4` | `0x0002b9e4`, `0x0002ba6a`, `0x0002bb3e` |
| ICI0A secondary compare, GR11B | `0x0002a164`, `0x0002a1fa` |
| Secondary high-byte POPCR `0xfffff700` writes | `0x0002bb7a`, `0x0002a194`, `0x0002a22a` |

The lower and upper bytes of POPCR select the rising and falling
PULS actions. Application vector `0x000202e4` selects IMI11A
`0x0002b7ac`. With a primary compare armed, it clears the low byte
at `0x0002b818`. The alternate rearm path can program GR11A at
`0x0002b8d8` and restore the low-byte action at `0x0002b8fe`.
Application vector `0x000202ec` selects IMI11B
`0x0002bbc0` on both ROMs. The match handler saves the high byte
and clears it at `0x0002bc1a` while preserving the low byte. Its
alternate rearm path can program GR11B at `0x0002bfa8` and restore
the high-byte action mask at `0x0002bfe0`.

## JK78C

Application vector `0x00020154` points to ICI0A at `0x0002b8fa`.
The tooth-7 route tests `0xffff8509 & 0x80` before calling the
feeder at `0x0002c3fe`. The feeder at `0x0002095c` indexes nine
longword targets from the table at `0x00021c8c`.
The fifth word at `0x00021c9c` selects the launcher at `0x00069a80`.
The launcher runs the correction and final timing builders before
the primary command routine and ends in the secondary command
routine.

| Ignition operation | JK78C |
| --- | --- |
| Final timing builder and output | `0x000693b8` -> `0xffff1f45` |
| APC command builder and stores | `0x0006981e`, `0x00069938/0x00069948` -> `0xffff11cf` |
| Primary scheduler call and argument extension | `0x0006994c/0x0006994e` -> `0x0002d6f6` |
| Secondary routine and scheduler | `0x00069958`, `0x00069992` -> `0x0002db52` |
| Primary count and fractional byte | `0xffff152e`, `0xffff1530` |
| Secondary count and fraction pairs | `0xffff1440/0xffff1442`, `0xffff1446/0xffff1447` |
| Retained timestamp and normalized period | `0xffff1560`, `0xffff220c` |
| Immediate and deferred primary GR11A stores | `0x0002d8de`, `0x0002bdde` |
| Secondary scheduler and deferred GR11B stores | `0x0002dd60`, `0x0002bf68/0x0002bffa` |
| POPCR low-byte and high-byte updates | `0x0002d914/0x0002be06`, `0x0002dd9e/0x0002bf98/0x0002c02a` |

The direct command branch subtracts the signed timing byte from
`ROM8[0x0000838f] = 110`. The secondary command subtracts the
interval byte at `0xffff22ca` from 120, applies the optional trim
and lower bound, stores its low byte at `0xffff2184`, and adds the
two unsigned command bytes for the tail transfer. The secondary
scheduler alternates its slots through bit 7 of `0xffff8503`.
The normal deferred compare uses the retained timestamp plus the
bounded fractional delay, modulo 65536. Application vector
`0x000202e4` selects IMI11A `0x0002d982`, which clears the
armed low-byte action at `0x0002da10`. The alternate rearm path
can set GR11A at `0x0002daca` and restore the low-byte action
at `0x0002daf2`.

### Secondary match and dwell timeout

Application vector `0x000202ec` selects IMI11B at `0x0002dde4`.
The handler acknowledges the match in TSR11 with `0x0101` at
`0x0002de2e` before testing `0xffff851c` bit 7. With the bit set, it
saves the high byte of POPCR to `0xffff13c8` at `0x0002de5a` and
clears that byte at `0x0002de60`, preserving the low byte.

With `0xffff44f3` bit 6 clear, the handler selects the next dwell
mask from the ROM table at `0x000226ef` and publishes it to
`0xffff13cb`. Bit 7 of `0xffff8504` selects index `0xffff1650`
and is consumed by that branch. The other branch uses `0xffff13cd`.
The handler clamps the sum of the dwell angle at `0xffff22ca` and
the stock offsets 30 and 20 at `0x0000880b` and `0x000088ac` to 255. It
stores the result at `0xffff1320` and computes the timeout from
`angle * RAM32[0xffff11e8] / 1200`, capped at `ROM16[0x00008f18]`
(3125) and multiplied by ten. With `0xffff44f3` bit 6 set it
zeros the next mask and skips the timeout-arm branch.

For a nonzero saved mask the handler alternates the timeout slot
through bit 7 of `0xffff851e`. The compare is based on GR11B,
with a minimum of two counts beyond the current TCNT5. The mode
bit for each slot distinguishes a finite compare from an
extended timeout. The stock cap limits this arming calculation to
31250 counts, so it selects a finite compare. The extended path
for delays of at least 65536 counts stores the high word of the
delay and an absolute deadline based on `TCNT0H >> 5`.

| Timeout operation | Slot A | Slot B |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Saved mask in slot | `0xffff13c9` | `0xffff13ca` |
| Mode bit 7 and extension word | `0xffff8519`, `0xffff131c` | `0xffff851a`, `0xffff131e` |
| Extended deadline | `0xffff1438` | `0xffff143c` |
| Compare register and initial store | `0xfffff4e2`, `0x0002dfce` | `0xfffff4e4`, `0x0002e040` |
| Vector word and interrupt entry | `0x00020224 -> 0x0002e266` | `0x00020228 -> 0x0002e3c0` |
| Acknowledge / disable interrupt | `0x0002e28c/0x0002e2a2` | `0x0002e3e6/0x0002e3fc` |
| Extended compare store | `0x0002e2fe` | `0x0002e46c` |
| GR11A stores at finite expiry | `0x0002e352`, `0x0002e38a` | `0x0002e4c0`, `0x0002e4f8` |
| POPCR low-byte action stores at finite expiry | `0x0002e35e`, `0x0002e39a` | `0x0002e4cc`, `0x0002e508` |

An extended timeout decrements the extension word and re-enables
the compare. It uses a delay of `0xffff` until the last extension,
when it uses the unsigned remaining absolute time capped at
`0xffff` and selects finite mode. In finite mode the interrupt
checks that the slot mask is nonzero and the selected PULS input
remains low. With a pending `0xffff152e` primary tooth count, it
clears the count, writes `GR11A = TCNT11 + 2`, sets the low POPCR
mask and sets `0xffff851b` bit 7. With no pending count it sets the
same POPCR mask and moves GR11A only when its unsigned distance
from TCNT11 exceeds five counts or `0xffff13cc` is nonzero.

IMI11B also checks the next mask against the successor of its
previous value. While `0xffff84f2` bit 7 is set and `0xffff13cc`
is zero, a mismatch or two zero masks set `0xffff8515` bits 7
and 6. Otherwise it moves bit 7 into bit 6 and clears bit 7.
It then consumes `0xffff851c` bit 7. The fallback rearm
requires `0xffff241e` bit 6, either bit 5 or 6 in `0xffff851c`,
and zero countdown in the slot selected by `0xffff8503` bit 7
XOR `0xffff851c` bit 6. It computes the slot fraction times
`RAM16[0xffff220c]` divided by ten and subtracts the unsigned
16-bit difference between the completed GR11B match and the
selected cam capture. A zero `0xffff14b4` selects GR1E and a
nonzero value selects GR1F. The handler clamps the result to
`0..65535` and adds it to the retained timestamp at `0xffff1560`,
with a minimum of two counts beyond TCNT11. It writes GR11B at
`0x0002e1d2` and clears the match flag.
When `0xffff84f2` and `0xffff44e0` both have bit 7 set,
`0xffff13cc` is zero and `0xffff8515` bit 7 is clear, it ORs
`0xffff13cb << 8` into POPCR at `0x0002e20c`. Then it sets
`0xffff851c` bit 7 and clears bits 5 and 6.

The compare register and mask paths establish the software output.
Output-to-coil continuity and mechanical timing remain properties
of the exact hardware.
